# SAVIOR-Warehouse: a synthetic stereo-inertial warehouse dataset with controllable dynamic content

SAVIOR-Warehouse is rendered with NVIDIA Isaac Sim 6.0. It is the motivating scenario of the SAVIOR paper: an
indoor warehouse with racks, pallets, boxes, trolleys, guard rails and windows, where people walk and people drive
forklifts that carry boxes. The interaction chain is person → forklift → box/pallet. Driverless forklifts and
stacked pallets/boxes are also present; they are potentially dynamic, but here they are static.

Every trajectory is rendered at **four dynamic levels L0–L3** with the **same static scene, the same camera
trajectory and the same IMU noise realisation**. Differences between levels therefore come from the moving objects
only. The levels cover the regime where dynamic-object masking can matter for VIO: on uHumans2, humans cover under
1 % of the image (results/p3a). Here, the moving-object coverage goes up to about 25 % of the image, with long,
coherent tracks of large objects that co-move with the camera.

* Sensor: a ZED 2i-like rectified stereo pair. It runs at 672×376 and 20 Hz with a 0.12 m baseline, global shutter
  and no distortion. Default optics are the 4 mm lens (fx = 470 px, 71°×44°); the 2.1 mm wide-angle rig is an option.
  An analytic 200 Hz IMU comes with a documented ZED 2i-class noise model, including the bias truth.
* Ground truth: exact poses (IMU and camera), stereo instance segmentation (uint16) with an
  instance → class map, a per-frame **moving** flag for each instance (true speed > 0.05 m/s), depth, and per-frame
  moving-object coverage.
* ROS 2 bags (mcap, zstd) contain mono8 stereo, camera_info, the noisy and clean IMU, colour-coded class
  segmentation (all objects, and moving objects only) and the GT pose. The bags come with OpenVINS configs and oracle
  colour maps for `savior_perception`.
* A domain-randomised segmentation training set (COCO polygons, 8 classes) is provided for YOLO26-seg fine-tuning.

Size: about 18 GB in total (raw 12 GB, ROS 2 bags 5.3 GB, segmentation set 0.5 GB).

Everything is generated by the scripts in this directory. Scenes and actors are fully scripted in time, so a
sequence re-renders deterministically.

Notes on the table:
* Coverage is the fraction of cam0 pixels on instances that move in that frame (true speed > 0.05 m/s).
* "FAST features on moving objects" is the share of OpenVINS-like FAST corners (threshold 20, 150 points on a 5×5
  grid, every 5th frame) that land on moving objects, from `tools/coverage_report.py`. It is a proxy for the share
  of filter measurements on dynamic objects; results/p3a found that masking needs about 10 % or more.
* Stock OpenVINS degrades with the dynamic level. In the hall, at L3, the forklift co-moving 3.7 m ahead and the
  ones driving alongside make the filter "stand still": the estimated speed drops to about 0.05 m/s at 10-25 s. ATE
  reaches 5 m, and one run diverges.
* With perfect moving-object masks (`dataset:=warehouse_moving`, roles warehouse_oracle_moving), SAVIOR is back at
  the L0 level, with 4352/4352 masks exactly synced.
* The ATE is the posyaw RMSE of stock OpenVINS (`config/warehouse`, deterministic replay), at bag start offsets
  0.0 / 0.3 / 0.6 s. It is a sanity check, not a benchmark: run-to-run sensitivity is large, as in results/p3a.
* Scenes A and B use the same environment, `Isaac/Environments/Simple_Warehouse/full_warehouse.usd`, but different
  areas, layouts and trajectories.

**Scene A, "aisles"** (paper scenario 4 spirit: objects crossing the camera path). The camera takes off in the hall
and flies through the north rack aisles in an 89 m loop: up aisle x = −12.9, west along the north cross aisle, down
aisle x = −17.9, east along the south cross aisle, and up aisle x = −7.9.
* L1: people cross in front of the camera, walk head-on and walk ahead in the aisles; one person stands still.
* L2: forklifts with drivers and boxes cross the hall and both cross aisles, one overtakes in parallel, and two lead
  the camera 8–11 m ahead.
* L3: forklifts drive 3.7 m ahead of the camera through all three aisles (co-moving), one drives alongside in the
  hall, and more people are added.

**Scene B, "hall"** (paper scenario 5 spirit: objects moving parallel to the camera). The hall has a block-stacking
and staging layout: two pallet islands split by a crossing gap, a staging area with parked forklifts, rails and
windows. The camera flies a clockwise 96 m loop: lane N, lane E, lane S, lane W, then the middle lane.
* L1: people walk ahead or head-on along every lane and cross the middle lane; one person stands still.
* L2: a forklift leads the camera 8.6 m ahead around the whole loop. Other forklifts pass in the opposite direction
  on lanes N and E, one crosses all lanes at take-off, and a slow one is overtaken on lane S.
* L3: a forklift leads the camera 3.7 m ahead around the whole loop. Forklifts drive alongside the camera, 2.2–2.6 m
  to the side, on lanes N and W and on the middle lane.

The levels are nested: Lk contains every actor of L(k−1). L0 contains only the static layout, with parked
driverless forklifts and stacked pallets and boxes.

## Sensor rig, frames and calibration

* World frame W: the Isaac stage frame (Z up, metres, gravity −9.81 m/s² along Z).
* IMU / body frame I: FLU, i.e. x forward, y left, z up.
* Cameras C0/C1: OpenCV convention (x right, y down, z forward), with `R_CtoI = [[0,0,1],[-1,0,0],[0,-1,0]]`.
  cam0 sits at `p_C0inI = (0, 0.06, 0)` and cam1 at `(0, −0.06, 0)`, so the baseline is 0.12 m. The extrinsics are
  exact, and the camera and IMU share one clock (time offset 0).
* Intrinsics (4 mm rig): `[fx, fy, cx, cy] = [470, 470, 336, 188]`, 672×376, no distortion (rectified).
* Calibration files ship with every sequence in `raw/<seq>/calib/`, together with the rig yaml. They are in OpenVINS
  kalibr format, `kalibr_imucam_chain.yaml` and `kalibr_imu_chain.yaml`, and are generated from the rig yaml
  (wh/rig.py).
* Rendering: RTX real-time renderer (RaytracedLighting) with 4 subframes per frame. There is no motion blur and no
  auto-exposure, so left and right exposures are identical and constant.
* Camera poses are applied to USD cameras whose aperture and focal length reproduce fx exactly. This was verified as
  follows (tools/check_geometry.py):
  * stereo warping with the GT depth has its photometric minimum at disparity scale 1.00 and offset 0 px;
  * re-projecting between frames with the GT poses beats ±1 cm / ±0.1° pose perturbations.

## Trajectory and IMU

**Trajectory.** The body trajectory is a clamped **quintic B-spline (C⁴)** in position plus a quintic yaw spline.
They are least-squares fitted to a reference that is built as follows:
* a 2D polyline at 1 m/s cruise speed, slower at corners;
* a smooth ±0.25 m height profile;
* a ±12° yaw "look-around";
* a 0.3 m take-off climb.

The yaw rate is capped at ≤ 100 °/s by widening the knot spacing. The first and last 5 control points are pinned, so
the drone **hovers exactly still** for 3 s before take-off and 2 s at the end, which gives a clean static
initialisation window. Knots are denser (0.2 s) near both ends.

**Attitude.** Attitude follows quadrotor differential flatness: the body z axis is aligned with a + g e₃, and yaw is
taken from the spline. The angular velocity uses the exact closed form: z_b' = (I − z_b z_bᵀ) j / |a + g e₃|,
p = −y_b·z_b', q = x_b·z_b', and r from d/dt(y_b·x_c) = 0. An AMR mode (roll = pitch = 0) is available
(`platform: amr`).

**IMU, generated analytically** at 200 Hz on an exact integer-ns grid (camera = every 10th IMU sample):
`gyro = ω_B`, `accel = Rᵀ(a − g_W)`.

Note that for an ideal quadrotor the specific force always lies along body z: `acc_x = acc_y = 0`, and horizontal
accelerations appear as tilt. The take-off climb therefore provides the accelerometer excitation that OpenVINS'
static initialiser needs (accel std about 0.7 m/s² at take-off, 0.04 m/s² in hover).

**Noise model** (continuous time, Kalibr / OpenVINS convention; values are those of a ZED 2i-class Bosch BMI085 IMU):

| | white noise density | bias random walk | turn-on bias σ |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| gyroscope | 2.44e-4 rad/s/√Hz | 2.0e-5 rad/s²/√Hz | 3e-3 rad/s |
| accelerometer | 1.6e-3 m/s²/√Hz | 3.0e-4 m/s³/√Hz | 3e-2 m/s² |

The model is `noisy = clean + b + n`, with `n ~ N(0, σ²/Δt)` and `b_{k+1} = b_k + σ_b √Δt N(0, 1)`. The bias truth is
in `imu_bias.csv`. The seed is fixed per trajectory, so all levels of a trajectory have identical IMU data.

**Consistency check** (`tools/check_imu.py`, scene A; scene B is the same within rounding):

| check | aisles_t1 | hall_t1 |
|---|--:|--:|
| gyro_clean vs SO(3) central difference of GT (RMS / max) [rad/s] | 1.7e-05 / 4.3e-04 | 1.6e-05 / 4.3e-04 |
| acc_clean vs Rᵀ(d²p/dt² + g e₃) of GT (RMS / max) [m/s²] | 2.9e-05 / 3.0e-04 | 2.9e-05 / 3.1e-04 |
| GT velocity vs central difference of GT position (RMS) [m/s] | 2.4e-06 | 2.2e-06 |
| dead reckoning of clean IMU from GT state, 10 s windows: position error mean / max [m] | 2.5e-04 / 1.5e-03 | 1.4e-04 / 9.4e-04 |
| dead reckoning, rotation error max [deg] | 2.5e-04 | 1.9e-04 |
| gyro white noise density estimated / configured | 2.433e-04 / 2.440e-04 | 2.425e-04 / 2.440e-04 |
| accel white noise density estimated / configured | 1.596e-03 / 1.600e-03 | 1.604e-03 / 1.600e-03 |
| gyro / accel bias random walk estimated (configured 2.0e-5 / 3.0e-4) | 2.01e-05 / 3.00e-04 | 2.00e-05 / 3.00e-04 |
| max angular rate / max speed / mean moving speed / max tilt | 89 °/s / 1.09 / 0.89 m/s / 11.4° | 85 °/s / 1.05 / 0.89 m/s / 11.4° |

The residuals against the differentiated GT are the finite-difference error at 200 Hz; they are largest at take-off,
where the knots are dense. The analytic body rates were also compared with a 1 kHz numerical differentiation of the
spline attitude: the RMS difference is 1.6e-7 rad/s (max 4.4e-6, at take-off). The clean IMU therefore *is* the derivative of the GT, and its
error is 2-3 orders of magnitude below the white noise per sample (gyro 3.4e-3 rad/s, accel 2.3e-2 m/s² at 200 Hz).

## Raw sequence format (`raw/<seq>/`)

| file | content |
|---|---|

| `gt_imu_tum.txt` | TUM `t x y z qx qy qz qw`, T_world_imu at 200 Hz |

| `gt_imu_vel.csv` | world velocity and body rates at 200 Hz |
| `instances.json` | per instance: id, name, class, role, USD prim, actor, `moving_ever`, `moving_intervals_ns`, visible frames, colour |
| `moving.csv` | per frame: ids of the instances that move in that frame |
| `coverage.csv` | per frame: moving coverage (total and per class), person and potential-class coverage, # moving instances |
| `actors.csv` | per frame and actor: x, y, heading, speed (GT object motion) |
| `calib/` | kalibr_imucam_chain.yaml, kalibr_imu_chain.yaml, rig.yaml |
| `meta.json` | render settings, timing, coverage summary |

Instance ids:
* 1–2999: environment objects (racks, rack shields → guard_rail, pallets, boxes, the parked env forklift, a push
  cart → trolley);
* 3000–4999: added static props (pallet stacks, KLT bins → box, dollies → trolley, guard rails, windows);
* ≥ 5000: actors, in blocks of 16 per actor. A forklift's block holds the body, the pallet, the boxes and the driver
  at +10. Actor ids are stable across levels.

Classes (wh/classes.py) and their roles in roles/warehouse.yaml:
* static: rack, guard_rail, window;
* potentially dynamic: forklift, box, pallet, trolley;
* dynamic: person.

## ROS 2 bags (`ros2/<seq>/`)

Message stamps are the exact simulator stamps; the bag time equals the header stamp. Topics:
* `/cam0/image_raw`, `/cam1/image_raw`: mono8 images.
* `/cam0/camera_info`, `/cam1/camera_info`: `P[3] = −fx·b` on cam1.
* `/imu0` (noisy) and `/imu0_clean`.
* `/cam0/segmentation`: rgb8, every warehouse-class instance in its class colour.
* `/cam0/segmentation_moving`: rgb8, only the instances moving in that frame.
* `/gt/pose`: PoseStamped, 200 Hz.
* `/tf_static`: imu0 → cam0 and cam1.

Options: `--instances` adds `/cam0/instance_id` (mono16), and `--rgb` adds `/cam0/image_rgb`.

## OpenVINS / SAVIOR usage

Configs (generated by `tools/export_bringup_configs.py` from the rig yaml):
* `savior_bringup/config/warehouse/`: `estimator_config.yaml`, modelled on config/uhumans2. Stereo, `num_pts 150`,
  online calibration off, `init_imu_thresh 0.30`, dynamic init as a fallback. Also the kalibr files and
  `savior_perception.yaml`.
* `savior_bringup/config/oracle/warehouse_colormap.yaml`: class colours of `/cam0/segmentation`.
* `savior_bringup/config/oracle/warehouse_colormap_moving.yaml`: `moving_<class>` colours of
  `/cam0/segmentation_moving`.
* `savior_bringup/config/roles/warehouse_oracle_moving.yaml`: everything that moves is dynamic, which gives the
  "perfect motion mask" upper bound.

The launch preset `warehouse` (in baseline.launch.py and savior.launch.py) plays the topics
`/cam0/image_raw /cam1/image_raw /imu0 /tf_static`.

```bash
SHARE=$(ros2 pkg prefix savior_bringup)/share/savior_bringup
# stock OpenVINS
ros2 launch savior_bringup baseline.launch.py config:=warehouse \
    bag:=$WS/data/warehouse/ros2/hall_t1_L3 output:=$WS/results/.../stamped_traj_estimate.txt
# SAVIOR with oracle class masks (paper roles: person dynamic; forklift / box / pallet / trolley potential)
ros2 launch savior_bringup savior.launch.py config:=warehouse backend:=oracle_colormap \
    roles_file:=$SHARE/config/roles/warehouse.yaml bag:=... output:=...
# oracle MOVING masks (upper bound: exactly the moving instances are dynamic)
ros2 launch savior_bringup savior.launch.py config:=warehouse dataset:=warehouse_moving backend:=oracle_colormap \
    roles_file:=$SHARE/config/roles/warehouse_oracle_moving.yaml bag:=... output:=...
savior-eval single --est .../stamped_traj_estimate.txt --gt $WS/data/warehouse/gt/hall_t1_L3.txt --out ...
```

`tools/ov_sanity.sh <seq>` runs the three-offset sanity check. Each trial runs in an isolated container with no
network and its own ROS domain.

## Segmentation training set (`seg_train/`)

* **2380 train + 340 val RGB images** at 672×376, from 16 layout
  seeds. Every 8th seed is held out for val, so val layouts are unseen.
* Images are JPEG (q95), in `images/{train,val}/`. The uint16 instance masks are in `masks/{train,val}/`. COCO
  instance annotations (polygons, iscrowd 0, ≥ 40 px) are in `annotations/instances_{train,val}.json`; each image
  record also stores its seed, base layout, lighting, floor, fx and time.
* A YOLO-seg export is in `yolo/` (`data.yaml`, symlinked images, normalised polygons), made by
  `tools/coco_to_yolo.py`. It is ready for `yolo segment train data=.../yolo/data.yaml`.
* Categories: 1 rack, 2 guard_rail, 3 window, 4 forklift, 5 box, 6 pallet, 7 trolley, 8 person.
* Instances, train / val:

  | class | train | val |
  |---|--:|--:|
  | rack | 58655 | 7250 |
  | guard_rail | 8250 | 1160 |
  | window | 2357 | 348 |
  | forklift | 6785 | 1012 |
  | box | 174409 | 22369 |
  | pallet | 36377 | 4403 |
  | trolley | 813 | 165 |
  | person | 6721 | 1499 |

* Domain randomisation (`isaac/render_segtrain.py`, `scenes/segtrain.py`):
  * layout: base aisles or hall, plus 4–9 random forklifts (driverless or driven, with pallet, boxes or no load,
    random fork height) and 6–14 people (walking or idle, 10 characters);
  * 3 lighting presets (default, cool-bright, warm-dim) with ±25 % per-frame intensity jitter on every env light;
  * textures: floor MDL swap, wood MDLs on about 40 % of the pallets, cardboard and plastic tints on about 35 % of
    the boxes, rail colour;
  * camera poses on the free lanes, aimed at a random object 1.5–15 m away, with h 0.3–2.6 m, pitch −30..+10° and
    roll σ = 3°;
  * 70 % at fx = 470 px (4 mm lens) and 30 % at fx = 264 px (2.1 mm lens).
* Frames without a person, forklift or trolley are dropped with probability 0.6.
* Rendering took 26 min with 12 RTX subframes per image.
* Caveat: trolley is the rarest class (813 train instances). Boxes are dominated by small
  boxes on the racks.

## How to regenerate

Requirements: the host Isaac Sim 6.0.1 (`$HOME/isaacsim`, override the launcher with `ISAAC_PY`; headless), the local asset pack
(`SAVIOR_ISAAC_ASSETS`, default `$HOME/isaac_sim_assets/Assets/Isaac/6.0`; no Nucleus or network), and the
SAVIOR Docker image for the ROS conversion and the OpenVINS runs. Only one Isaac process runs at a time.

```bash
cd src/savior/savior_datasets/warehouse
ISAAC=${ISAAC_PY:-$HOME/isaacsim/python.sh}
$ISAAC isaac/bake_people.py                                    # once: retarget walk / idle / sit clips per character
$ISAAC isaac/render_sequence.py --scene hall --level 3 --plan  # plan view + clearance report (no rendering)
$ISAAC isaac/render_sequence.py --scene hall --level 3 --preview 4 --scale 0.25   # quick coverage estimate
tools/render_all.sh                                            # render + convert all 8 sequences (Docker for ROS)
$ISAAC tools/check_imu.py $WS/data/warehouse/raw/<seq>         # IMU <-> GT consistency
$ISAAC tools/check_geometry.py $WS/data/warehouse/raw/<seq>    # render <-> calibration <-> GT
$ISAAC tools/make_previews.py $WS/data/warehouse/raw/*  ;  $ISAAC tools/coverage_report.py $WS/data/warehouse/raw/*
$ISAAC isaac/render_segtrain.py                                # segmentation training set
python3 tools/export_bringup_configs.py                        # savior_bringup configs from the rig yaml
```

`render_sequence.py` takes `--rig configs/rig_zed2i_2mm.yaml` for the wide-angle variant; output is written to
`raw/<seq>`, so use `--out` to keep both variants. Rendering runs at about 5.5 stereo frames/s on an RTX PRO 6000:
about 7 min for a 110 s sequence including depth.

**How people are animated.**
* The Isaac People characters (101-joint Reallusion skeleton) get the `People/Animations` clips (walk loop in
  place, idle loop, sit). The clips are retargeted offline with `omni.anim.retarget`; the source skeleton is the IRA
  test biped `isaacsim.replicator.agent.core/data/test_scenes/test_biped/biped_demo_meters.usd`.
* Each frame, the renderer samples the clip into a per-character `SkelAnimation`. Walking is foot-locked: the phase
  is the walked distance divided by the clip speed of 1.083 m/s. The pose blends into the idle clip when the person
  slows down.
* Drivers use the last frame of the sit clip on the forklift seat.
* No animation graph, timeline or physics is involved, so renders are deterministic.

**How actors move.** Actor paths are scripted per scene (`PathBuilder`): waits, polyline legs and camera-relative
"follow" segments for co-moving vehicles, Gaussian-smoothed in time. Forklifts move rigidly, with the driver and the
load attached.

**Collision checks.** `--plan` checks the actor paths against the camera and each other with 3-disc forklift
footprints. It also checks them against a triangle-rasterised occupancy map of the static scene. The plan images are
in `preview/plan_*.png`.

  Each module documents its choreography and has a `HARD_CASES` dict per level (used by the manifest).
* Same rig (4 mm), 3 s hover + take-off, analytic IMU. New paths with speed changes (0.7-1.3 m/s per leg), turns

## Confirmatory split t3 (procedural, 4 environments)

A second, larger test split for confirmatory claims: **24 independent trajectories** (6 per environment) x L0-L3 =
96 sequences. The design (seeds, environments, levels, rig, IMU noise, duration, exclusion rule and the pre-registered
analysis) was frozen in `experiments/t3_confirmatory/DESIGN.yaml` before anything was rendered; its md5 is in
`experiments/t3_confirmatory/DESIGN.md5`. **Do not tune, select or debug
on t3.** Sequences are `<id>_t3_L<k>` with ids `fw01..fw06`, `sw01..`, `ms01..`, `wx01..`.

Environments (Isaac Sim 6.0 asset pack, `SAVIOR_ISAAC_ASSETS`):

| site | USD | in t1/t2 | layout |
|---|---|---|---|
| fw | `Simple_Warehouse/full_warehouse.usd` (south hall) | yes (other layouts) | seeded lane grid, 30 x 29 m |
| sw | `Simple_Warehouse/warehouse.usd` | no | seeded lane grid in the 20 x 30 m shell (one wall rack row) |
| ms | `Simple_Warehouse/warehouse_multiple_shelves.usd` | no | loop through both rack aisles; pallet rows at the rack faces (its pallet bins deactivated) |
| wx | `warehouse_20x20_envi/warehouse_20x20_envi.usd` | no | other asset family (white / blue hall, blue wall racks); central clutter deactivated, seeded layout |

**Generator** (`scenes/procedural.py`, numpy only; `isaac/render_sequence.py --scene procedural:<id>`; development
seeds as `procedural:dev-<site>-<seed>`). Per seed, separate RNG streams for:
* the static layout: ring (perimeter) lanes plus seeded inner lanes (width 3.9-5.2 m by site), wall storage strips,
  blocks filled with pallet stacks (1-3 box layers, two box sizes), KLT pallets, pallet piles, dollies (trolley),
  barrels, cones, guard rails, and parked driverless forklifts with raised loads. Only segmenter classes are used.
* the camera: a random tour on the lane graph (no U-turns, unvisited lanes preferred), 80-92 m, 92-112 s. Legs run
  at 0.70-1.30 m/s and 1.0-1.8 m high, with one leg at 2.2-2.4 m. The t1/t2 quadrotor model is unchanged: 3 s hover +
  take-off climb, 2 s end hover.
* the actors, generated L3 co-movers -> L2 -> L1 -> other L3 extras. Each one is accepted only if it stays clear of
  the camera (>= 0.25 m), the static layout and all actors already accepted. The levels are therefore nested and
  collision-free by construction:
  * L1: walking people;
  * L2: forklifts with drivers and loads, plus people pushing loaded carts (`Forklift(kind='cart')`, dolly = trolley);
  * L3: co-moving forklifts 3-5.5 m ahead (parked ahead during the hover), alongside forklifts, loaded carts.
  Moving actors get automatic visibility windows: they appear out of view before their onset and disappear out of
  view after they stop.

Additive code changes, with t1/t2 byte-identical (hash of the camera, IMU and every actor / prop of all five t1/t2
modules, before vs after):
* `Scene.env_label_mode='recursive'` (nested env labelling);
* `Forklift.kind='cart'`;
* `Person.attached_to`;
* `render_sequence.py --scene module:arg`, cart footprint, pusher pairs skipped in the clearance check.

Render + convert (raw and bags on the HDD archive, symlinked into `data/warehouse/raw` and `ros2_stereoseg`):
`tools/render_t3.sh fw01:0 fw01:3 ...`. Checks, sanity, exclusions and the evaluation scripts are in
`results/t3_confirmatory/` (see its REPORT.md).

## Limitations

* No rotor drag, vibration, rolling shutter, motion blur, lens distortion or exposure changes. The images are clean
  and rectified. These effects could be added for robustness studies.
* Forklift wheels do not turn: the asset's body and wheels are a single mesh. Forks are raised statically.
* People animation is limited to walking, idling and sitting, so there is no carrying or picking.
* There is no optical flow; Replicator's `motion_vectors` annotator could be added to `render_sequence.py`.
* "window" instances are wall-mounted Office window assets, placed against the opaque warehouse walls.
